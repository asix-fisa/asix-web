Overview¶

ASIX will deliver a new class of hybrid, room-temperature X-ray detectors for single-photon, event-driven, spectral imaging. The detectors will have both high spatial and spectral resolution and will be suitable for large data throughput applications for space observations and material science. This will be achieved by coupling custom, pixelated, large area front-end ASICs, equipped with analog readout and smart self-triggering logic, to finely segmented silicon sensors with similar patterns, both down to a 50 μm hexagonal pitch. The project will engineer, qualify, deliver and document detector prototypes up to a Technology Readiness Level (TRL) of 6, and develop the associated readout electronics and software.

Heritage¶

The project builds upon the heritage of our team in the development and operation of detectors and detector systems for space applications, most notably the Gas Pixel Detectors for the Imaging X-ray Polarimetry Explorer (IXPE) mission, described in Baldini et al. 2021.

The ASIX prototype¶

We have successfully assembled and tested prototypes of the ASIX demonstrator by leveraging the XPOL-III analog readout ASIC described in Minuti et al. 2022, coupled to high-efficiency, solid state photon converters through standard bump-bonding techniques. These prototypes serve a number of different purposes: validate the basic design principles and our Monte Carlo simulation, consolidate the sensor requirements, measure relevant performance metrics (e.g. the effect of the charge sharing and the scaling of the energy and spatial resolution resolution with the thickness of the sensor and the pixel noise), and define a set of different configurations tailored to specific applications—e.g., optimized for the best possible spectral or imaging performance. They provide the natural test bed for the development and optimization of the data-acquisition system and the associated data processing software. In addition, we are using these first prototypes to inform and guide the implementation of the detectors verification strategy, and ultimately the definition of the final design to be engineered and qualified up to TRL 6 (system prototype demonstration in the relevant environment).

More information about the performance of the prototypes can be found in the publication page.
